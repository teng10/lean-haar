1.2 First Fundamental Theorem of Invariant Theory

To prove the hard direction of Schur-Weyl duality, we first prove that the centralizer of the permutation image is contained in the span of the diagonal image. This is a form of the First Fundamental Theorem (FFT) of invariant theory.

Definition 1.2.1 Tensor Basis (Primed)

SchurWeyl.tensorBasis’def:TensVThe standard basis of \(V^{\otimes k}\) constructed as the tensor product of the standard basis of \(V\):

\begin{equation} (e_I)_{I \in [d]^k} \end{equation}

Definition 1.2.2 Endomorphism Matrix Representation (Primed)

SchurWeyl.toEndMatrix’def:tensorBasis’, def:TensVThe linear equivalence between the endomorphism algebra \(\text{End}(V^{\otimes k})\) and the algebra of \(d^k \times d^k\) complex matrices:

\begin{equation} \text{End}(V^{\otimes k}) \simeq \text{Mat}_{d^k \times d^k}(\mathbb {C}) \end{equation}

Lemma 1.2.3 Matrix Representation of Diagonal Action (Primed)

SchurWeyl.toEndMatrix’_diagActiondef:toEndMatrix’, def:diagActionFor any operator \(g \in \text{End}(V)\), the matrix elements of its diagonal action \(g^{\otimes k}\) in the tensor basis are given by:

\begin{equation} (g^{\otimes k})_{I, J} = \prod _{m=1}^k g_{I(m), J(m)} \end{equation}

Lemma 1.2.4 Permutation Action on Tensor Basis (Primed)

SchurWeyl.permAction_tensorBasis’def:tensorBasis’, def:permAction, lem:permAction_tprodThe permutation action of \(W_\sigma \) on a basis element \(e_I\) permutes the indices:

\begin{equation} W_\sigma (e_I) = e_{I \circ \sigma ^{-1}} \end{equation}

\begin{equation} (W_\sigma )_{I, J} = \delta _{I, J \circ \sigma ^{-1}} \end{equation}

Lemma 1.2.10 DiagAction of Diagonal Operator

SchurWeyl.diagAction_diagonaldef:toEndMatrix, def:diagAction,lem:toEndMatrix_diagActionIf \(g \in \text{End}(V)\) is a diagonal operator with entries \(f\), then \(g^{\otimes k}\) is a diagonal operator with entries \(\prod _{m=1}^k f(I(m))\).

Lemma 1.2.11 Monomial Coefficient Independence

monomial_coeff_zero_of_eval_zeroIf a linear combination of distinct monomials in \(d^2\) variables over \(\mathbb {C}\) evaluates to zero at all points, then all coefficients are zero.

Definition 1.2.12 Pair Count Coincidence

SchurWeyl.pairCountFor two index functions \(I, J: [k] \to [d]\), the coincidence count \(\text{pairCount}(I, J)\) is a function counting how many indices \(m\) have \((I(m), J(m)) = (a, b)\) for each \((a, b) \in [d] \times [d]\).

Lemma 1.2.13 Product Monomial Representation

SchurWeyl.prod_eq_monomial_evaldef:pairCountThe product of matrix elements can be written as a monomial evaluation of the pair count:

\begin{equation} \prod _{m=1}^k g_{I(m), J(m)} = \prod _{(a,b)} g_{a,b}^{\text{pairCount}(I,J)(a,b)} \end{equation}

Lemma 1.2.14 Matrix Orbit Invariance

SchurWeyl.matrix_orbit_invariantdef:permImage, def:toEndMatrix’, lem:permAction_tensorBasis’, def:permAction, def:tensorBasis’If an operator \(X \in \text{End}(V^{\otimes k})\) commutes with all permutation operators \(W_\sigma \), then its matrix entries in the computational basis satisfy \(X_{I \circ \sigma , J \circ \sigma } = X_{I, J}\) for all \(\sigma \in S_k\).

Lemma 1.2.15 Pair Count Permutation Invariance

SchurWeyl.pairCount_permdef:pairCountPermuting the indices by \(\sigma \) preserves the pair count:

\begin{equation} \text{pairCount}(I \circ \sigma , J \circ \sigma ) = \text{pairCount}(I, J) \end{equation}

Lemma 1.2.16 Sum Grouped by Pair Count

SchurWeyl.sum_group_by_pairCountlem:prod_eq_monomial_eval, def:pairCountA double sum over indices can be grouped by the coincidence type (pair count).

Lemma 1.2.17 Linear Functional Vanishing

SchurWeyl.sum_vanishes_of_coeff_and_constdef:pairCountIf a matrix is constant on coincidence types, and the coincidence-type sums of coefficients vanish, then the total inner product sum vanishes.

Lemma 1.2.18 Functional Separation

SchurWeyl.exists_functional_separationIf \(x\) is a vector in a finite-dimensional space \(V\) not belonging to a submodule \(S\), there exists a linear functional \(\varphi \in V^*\) vanishing on \(S\) but such that \(\varphi (x) \neq 0\).
