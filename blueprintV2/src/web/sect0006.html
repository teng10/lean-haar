2.1 Solvability of the Weingarten System

We study the moment computations of operators under the Haar measure, which reduces to solving a system of linear equations. We first analyze this system in an abstract Hilbert-space setting.

Theorem 2.1.1 Abstract Gram-System Solvability

SchurWeyl.gram_system_solvableLet \(H\) be a finite-dimensional complex Hilbert space, \(v_1, \dots , v_n \in H\), and \(w \in H\). The linear system:

\begin{equation} \langle v_j, w \rangle = \sum _{i=1}^n c_i \langle v_j, v_i \rangle \quad \text{for all } j \end{equation}
1

always has at least one solution \(c \in \mathbb {C}^n\), which is given by the coefficients of the orthogonal projection of \(w\) onto \(\text{Span}(v_1, \dots , v_n)\).

To apply this to operators on \(V^{\otimes k}\), we define permutation operators and their adjoints.

Definition 2.1.2 Permutation Operators

SchurWeyl.permOpdef:permActionThe permutation operator \(V_d(\sigma ) \in \text{End}(V^{\otimes k})\) is the linear map corresponding to \(\sigma \in S_k\).

Definition 2.1.3 Adjoint Permutation Operators

SchurWeyl.permDualdef:permActionThe dual permutation operator \(V_d^\dagger (\sigma )\) is defined as the permutation operator of \(\sigma ^{-1}\).

Theorem 2.1.4 Hermitian Adjoint Agreement

SchurWeyl.permDual_eq_conjTransposedef:permOp, def:permDualIn the computational basis of \(V^{\otimes k}\), the matrix representation of \(V_d^\dagger (\sigma )\) is the conjugate transpose of the matrix representation of \(V_d(\sigma )\).

Definition 2.1.5 Matrix Vectorization

SchurWeyl.endVecThe vectorization \(\text{endVec}(O)\) of an operator \(O \in \text{End}(V^{\otimes k})\) is the vector of its matrix entries in the computational basis, viewed as an element of the Euclidean space.

Theorem 2.1.6 The Trace Bridge

SchurWeyl.inner_endVec_perm_eq_tracedef:permOp, def:permDual, def:endVecThe Euclidean inner product of the vectorizations of \(V_d(\sigma )\) and \(O\) equals the trace of their product:

\begin{equation} \langle \text{endVec}(V_d(\sigma )), \text{endVec}(O) \rangle = \text{Tr}(V_d^\dagger (\sigma ) O) \end{equation}
2

Theorem 2.1.7 Solvability of Weingarten Linear System

SchurWeyl.weingarten_linear_system_solvablethm:gram_system_solvable, thm:inner_endVec_perm_eq_traceFor any operator \(O \in \text{End}(V^{\otimes k})\), the Weingarten system of \(k!\) equations:

\begin{equation} \text{Tr}(V_d^\dagger (\sigma ) O) = \sum _{\pi \in S_k} c_\pi \text{Tr}(V_d^\dagger (\sigma ) V_d(\pi )) \quad \text{for all } \sigma \in S_k \end{equation}
3

always has at least one solution \(c: S_k \to \mathbb {C}\).

Lemma 2.1.8 Adjoint Trace Linearity

SchurWeyl.trace_permDual_comp_sumdef:permDual, def:permOpThe trace against \(V_d^\dagger (\sigma )\) is linear in combinations of permutation operators.

Lemma 2.1.9 Operator Span Inclusion

SchurWeyl.sum_smul_permOp_mem_spandef:permOp, def:permImageA linear combination of permutation operators lies in their span.

Theorem 2.1.10 Existence of Moment Representation Operator

SchurWeyl.weingarten_moment_operator_specthm:weingarten_linear_system_solvable, lem:sum_smul_permOp_mem_span, lem:trace_permDual_comp_sumThere exists a linear combination \(M = \sum _{\pi } c_\pi V_d(\pi )\) lying in \(\text{Span}(\mathcal{P}_{d,k})\) whose traces match \(O\):

\begin{equation} \text{Tr}(V_d^\dagger (\sigma ) M) = \text{Tr}(V_d^\dagger (\sigma ) O) \quad \text{for all } \sigma \in S_k \end{equation}
4

Theorem 2.1.11 Moment Operator Decomposition from Trace and Span Properties

SchurWeyl.weingarten_moment_decomposition_of_propslem:trace_permDual_comp_sum, def:permImage, def:permOpIf any operator \(M\) lies in \(\text{Span}(\mathcal{P}_{d,k})\) and has \(\text{Tr}(V_d^\dagger (\sigma ) M) = \text{Tr}(V_d^\dagger (\sigma ) O)\) for all \(\sigma \), then its coefficients solve the Weingarten system.
