1 Schur-Weyl Duality

1.1 Definitions and Commutants

Let \(V = \mathbb {C}^d\) denote the \(d\)-dimensional complex Euclidean space (modeled as Fin d \(\to \) \(\mathbb {C}\)). We study the \(k\)-fold tensor power of \(V\) and the actions of the symmetric group \(S_k\) and the endomorphism algebra \(\text{End}(V)\) on it.

Definition 1.1.1 Tensor Power Space
✓
#

Lean declarations

  • SchurWeyl.TensV

The \(k\)-fold tensor power of \(V\) is defined as:

\begin{equation} V^{\otimes k} = \bigotimes _{i=1}^k V \end{equation}

Definition 1.1.2 Permutation Action
✓
#

Uses

  • Definition 1.1.1

Lean declarations

  • SchurWeyl.permAction

Given a permutation \(\sigma \in S_k\), the permutation operator \(W_\sigma \) on \(V^{\otimes k}\) acts by permuting the tensor factors:

\begin{equation} W_\sigma (v_1 \otimes \dots \otimes v_k) = v_{\sigma ^{-1}(1)} \otimes \dots \otimes v_{\sigma ^{-1}(k)} \end{equation}

Definition 1.1.3 Diagonal Action
✓
#

Uses

Lean declarations

  • SchurWeyl.diagAction

Given an operator \(g \in \text{End}(V)\), the diagonal action \(g^{\otimes k}\) on \(V^{\otimes k}\) acts as \(g\) on each tensor factor:

\begin{equation} g^{\otimes k}(v_1 \otimes \dots \otimes v_k) = g(v_1) \otimes \dots \otimes g(v_k) \end{equation}

1.2 First Fundamental Theorem of Invariant Theory

To prove the hard direction of Schur-Weyl duality, we first prove that the centralizer of the permutation image is contained in the span of the diagonal image. This is a form of the First Fundamental Theorem (FFT) of invariant theory.
