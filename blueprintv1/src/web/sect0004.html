1.3 Permutation Invariance and Properties of Unitarily Invariant Measures

Theorem 1.3.1 Tensor power span theorem

Let \(k\) be a positive integer, and let \(X \in \mathbb {C}^[d]\). For any set \(\mathcal{A} \subseteq \mathbb {C}\) satisfying \(|\mathcal{A}| \geq k + 1\), it holds that

\begin{equation} \text{Span}\{ v^{\otimes k} : v \in \mathcal{A}^{[d]}\} =\mathcal{X}^{\mathbin {\bigcirc \mkern -15mu\vee }k}. \end{equation}
13

Proposition 1.3.2 Symmetric subspace and span of tensor product space

Let \(\mathcal{X}\) be a complex Euclidean space and \(k\) be a positive integer. Then the following statements are equivalent:

  1. \(X \in \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})^{\mathbin {\bigcirc \mkern -15mu\vee }k}\).

  2. For \(V \in U(\mathcal{X}^{\otimes k} \otimes \mathcal{X}^{\otimes k}, (\mathcal{X} \otimes \mathcal{X})^{\otimes k})\) being the isometry defined by

    \begin{equation} V \text{vec}(Y_1 \otimes \dots \otimes Y_k) = \text{vec}(Y_1) \otimes \dots \text{vec}(Y_n) \end{equation}
    14

    holding for all \(Y_1, \dots , Y_k \in \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})\), one has that

    \begin{equation} V \text{vec}(X) \in (\mathcal{X} \otimes \mathcal{X})^{\mathbin {\bigcirc \mkern -15mu\vee }k}. \end{equation}
    15

  3. \(X \in \text{Span}\{ Y^{\otimes k} : Y \in \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})\} \)

Theorem 1.3.3 Equivalence of symmetric subspace and unitary tensor span

prop:symLkSpanTensorProduct, thm:TensorPowerSpanTheorem Let \(\mathcal{X}\) be a complex Euclidean space and \(k\) be a positive integer. Then the symmetric subspace of linear operators is equivalent to the span of the unitary tensor products, i.e.,

\begin{equation} \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})^{\mathbin {\bigcirc \mkern -15mu\vee }k} = \text{Span}\{ U^{\otimes k} : U \in \mathcal{U}(\mathcal{X})\} . \end{equation}
16

Lemma 1.3.4 Invariant subspace lemma

Let \(\mathcal{X}\) be a complex Euclidean space, let \(\mathcal{V} \subseteq \mathcal{X}\) be a subspace, and let \(A \in \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})\) be an operator. The following statements are equivalent:

  1. It holds that both \(A\mathcal{V} \subseteq \mathcal{V}\) and \(A^{*}\mathcal{V} \subseteq \mathcal{V}\).

  2. It holds that \([A, \Pi _{\mathcal{V}}] = 0\).

Theorem 1.3.5 Double Commutant Theorem

lem:InvariantSubspace Let \(\mathcal{A}\) be a self-adjoint, unital subalgebra of \(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})\). Then the double commutant of \(\mathcal{A}\) is equal to \(\mathcal{A}\), i.e.,

\begin{equation} \text{Comm}(\text{Comm}(\mathcal{A})) = \mathcal{A}. \end{equation}
17

Proof ▼

For all \(X \in \text{Comm}(\mathcal{A})\) and any \(A \in \mathcal{A}\), we have

\begin{equation} [X, A] = AX - XA = 0. \end{equation}
18

Therefore, \(A \in \text{Comm}(\text{Comm}(\mathcal{A}))\), so \(\mathcal{A} \subseteq \text{Comm}(\text{Comm} (\mathcal{A}))\).

To demonstrate reverse inclusion, consider the subalgebra \(\mathcal{B} \subseteq \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X} \otimes \mathcal{X})\) defined as

\begin{equation} \mathcal{B} = \{ X \otimes \mathbb {I} : X \in \mathcal{A} \} . \end{equation}
19

For all \(Y \in \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X} \otimes \mathcal{X})\), it can be written in terms of the standard computational basis

\begin{equation} Y = \sum _{i, j \in [d]} \sum _{a, b \in [d]} c_{i, j, a, b} |i \rangle \langle j| \otimes |a \rangle \langle b|. \end{equation}
20

Using the definition of the standard computational basis of operators, we can rearrange the right-hand side into the form

\begin{equation} \label{eq:TensorDecomposition} Y = \sum _{a, b \in [d]} Y_{a, b} \otimes E_{a, b} \end{equation}
21

where \(Y_{a, b} = \sum _{i, j \in [d]} c_{i, j, a, b} |i \rangle \langle j |\).

\(Y\) commutes with \(\mathcal{B}\) if and only if \(Y(X \otimes \mathbb {I}) = (X \otimes \mathbb {I}) Y\) for all \(X \in \mathcal{A}\). Substituting Equation 21 and simplifying shows that \(Y\) commutes with \(\mathcal{B}\) if and only if \([Y_{a, b}, X] = 0\). For any \(X \in \text{Comm}(\text{Comm}(\mathcal{A}))\), \(X\) must commute with \(\text{Comm}(\mathcal{A})\), and therefore

\begin{equation} \label{eq:ccAImpliesccB} X \otimes \mathbb {I} \in \text{Comm}(\text{Comm}(\mathcal{B})). \end{equation}
22

It remains to show that every element in \(\text{Comm}(\text{Comm}(\mathcal{B}))\) is of the form \(X \otimes \mathbb {I}\). Define a subspace \(\mathcal{V} \subseteq X \otimes X\), where

\begin{equation} \mathcal{V} = \left\{ \text{vec}(X) = \sum _{a, b \in [d]} c_{a, b} |a \rangle \otimes |b \rangle : X \in \mathcal{A} \right\} . \end{equation}
23

For any arbitrary \(X \in \mathcal{A}\), we find that

\begin{equation} \begin{split} (X \otimes \mathbb {I}) \mathcal{V} = \{ (X \otimes \mathbb {I}) V : V \in \mathcal{V}\} \\ = \left\{ (X \otimes \mathbb {I}) \left( \sum _{a, b \in [d]} c_{a, b} |a \rangle \otimes |b \rangle \right) : \sum _{a, b \in [d]} c_{a, b} |a \rangle \langle b | \in \mathcal{A} \right\} \\ = \left\{ \sum _{a, b \in [d]} c_{a, b} X|a\rangle \otimes |b\rangle : \sum _{a, b \in [d]} c_{a, b} |a \rangle \langle b | \in \mathcal{A} \right\} \\ \subseteq \mathcal{V}, \end{split} \end{equation}
24

the inclusion of which follows from the fact that \(\mathcal{A}\) is an algebra so \(X|a\rangle = Xe_a \in \mathcal{A}\). Similarly, since \(\mathcal{A}\) is self-adjoint,

\begin{equation} (X^{*} \otimes \mathbb {I}) \mathcal{V} \subseteq \mathcal{V}. \end{equation}
29

By Lemma 1.3.4, this implies \([X \otimes \mathbb {I}, \Pi _{\mathcal{V}}] = 0\), and hence

\begin{equation} \label{eq:PiInCommB} \Pi _{\mathcal{V}} \in \text{Comm}(\mathcal{B}). \end{equation}
30

Therefore, for any arbitrary \(X \in \text{Comm}(\text{Comm}(\mathcal{A}))\), Equation 22 implies

\begin{equation} X \otimes \mathbb {I} \in \text{Comm}(\text{Comm}(\mathcal{B})). \end{equation}
31

It follows from Equation 30 that

\begin{equation} [X \otimes \mathbb {I}, \Pi _{\mathcal{V}}] = 0. \end{equation}
32

By Lemma 1.3.4, this yields

\begin{equation} (X \otimes \mathbb {I})\mathcal{V} \subseteq \mathcal{V}. \end{equation}
33

Since the subalgebra \(\mathcal{A}\) is unital, \(\mathbb {I} \in \mathcal{A}\) and thus \(\text{vec}(\mathbb {I}) \in \mathcal{V}\). Therefore,

\begin{equation} \begin{split} (X \otimes \mathbb {I}) \text{vec}(\mathbb {I}) = (X \otimes \mathbb {I}) \sum _{a \in [d]} |a\rangle \otimes |a \rangle \\ = \sum _{a \in [d]} X|a\rangle \otimes |a\rangle = \sum _{a \in [d]} \left( \sum _{b, c \in [d]} X_{b, c} |b \rangle \langle c| \right) |a \rangle \otimes |a \rangle \\ = \sum _{a \in [d]} \sum _{b \in [d]} X_{b, a} |b \rangle \otimes |a \rangle \\ = \sum _{b, a \in [d]} X_{b, a} |b \rangle \otimes |a \rangle = \text{vec}(X) \in \mathcal{V}. \end{split} \end{equation}
34

By the bijectivity of \(\text{vec}\), \(X \in \mathcal{A}\), so \(\text{Comm}(\text{Comm}(\mathcal{A})) \subseteq \mathcal{A}\).

Theorem 1.3.6 Schur-Weyl duality

prop:symLkSpanTensorProduct, def:SymmetricLinearOperator, thm:DoubleCommutant Let \(\mathcal{X}\) be a complex Euclidean space, \(k\) be a positive integer, and \(X \in \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X}^{\otimes k})\) be an operator. Then the set of all commutators of the unitary group is equivalent to the span of the permutation operators, i.e.,

\begin{equation} \text{Comm}(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{X}), k) = \text{Span}\{ W_{\pi } : \pi \in S_k \} \end{equation}
39

Proof ▼

Let \(X \in \text{Comm}(\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{X}), k)\). then by the bilinearity of the Lie bracket,

\begin{equation} X \in \text{Comm}(\text{Span}\{ U^{\otimes k} : U \in U(\mathcal{X}) \} ) \end{equation}
40

Proposition 1.3.2 implies that \(X \in \text{Comm}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})^{\mathbin {\bigcirc \mkern -15mu\vee }k})\), so

\begin{equation} \label{eq:CommSpanUnitaryEquivCommSymLk} \text{Comm}(\text{Span}\{ U^{\otimes k} : U \in U(\mathcal{X}) \} ) = \text{Comm}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})^{\mathbin {\bigcirc \mkern -15mu\vee }k}). \end{equation}
41

By Definition 1.2.1, \(Z \in \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})^{\mathbin {\bigcirc \mkern -15mu\vee }}\) if and only if there exists some \(X \in \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})\) such that

\begin{equation} Z = X = W_{\pi } X W_{\pi }^* \text{ for all } \pi \in S_k. \end{equation}
42

Right-multiplying and rearranging by \(W_\pi \) yields \(XW_{\pi } - W_{\pi } X = 0\), and so by the bilinearity of the Lie bracket,

\begin{equation} Z = X \in \text{Comm}(\text{Span}\{ W_{\pi } : \pi \in S_k\} ). \end{equation}
43

Hence

\begin{equation} \mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})^{\mathbin {\bigcirc \mkern -15mu\vee }k} = \text{Comm}(\text{Span}\{ W_{\pi } : \pi \in S_k\} ). \end{equation}
44

By Theorem 1.3.5, taking the commutant of both sides yields

\begin{equation} \text{Comm}(\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{X})^{\mathbin {\bigcirc \mkern -15mu\vee }k}) = \text{Span}\{ W_{\pi } : \pi \in S_k\} . \end{equation}
45

By substitution of Eq. 41, we have shown

\begin{equation} \text{Comm}(U(\mathcal{X}), k) = \text{Span}\{ W_{\pi } : \pi \in S_{k} \} . \end{equation}
46
